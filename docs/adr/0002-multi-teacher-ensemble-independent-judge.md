# 0002 - Multi-teacher ensemble with an independent judge

* Status: accepted
* Date: 2026-08-31

## Context and Problem Statement

Training examples are generated by LLMs, which hallucinate, disagree and share
biases. A single-teacher pipeline would bake unverified generations into the
student model. We need verified supervision without a human in the loop for
every record, at a cost compatible with hundreds of cases.

## Decision Drivers

* Hallucinated training examples poison the student; false positives must be
  caught before they reach `dataset.jsonl`.
* Commercial teachers are biased; a verifier from the same family shares the
  same blind spots.
* Cost must stay compatible with 20-2,000 case runs.
* Unresolved cases are acceptable: better rejected than wrong.

## Considered Options

* Single teacher, no verification
* Multi-teacher ensemble + judge from the same provider/family
* Multi-teacher ensemble + judge on a distinct provider and model family
* Multi-teacher with majority vote, no judge
* Human review of every record

## Decision Outcome

Chosen option: "Multi-teacher ensemble + judge on a distinct provider and
model family". Domain packs define teacher roles (security, coding) plus a
general critic; a judge receives case + RAG + all teacher analyses and produces
the canonical structured verdict. `config validate` enforces judge
independence by default: the judge role must resolve to a different provider
and a different declared `family` from every teacher role. The judge prompt
includes an adversarial pass: re-derive conclusions from the evidence, refuse
any claim not traceable to the case.

### Consequences

* Erroneous *agreement* (correlated bias) is addressed, not just disagreement:
  two same-family teachers can no longer confirm each other unchecked.
* Teacher/judge agreement rates are recorded per record and flagged in
  experiment reports when >= 95%.
* Cost scales with the number of roles; the `cheap`/`standard`/`maximum` modes
  exist for that reason.

### Confirmation

* `tests/unit/test_config.py` covers enforced/warn/off levels.
* `brainforge config validate` dry-builds providers and prints violations.
* `experiments/manager.py::high_agreement_warning` flags suspicious rates.

## Pros and Cons of the Options

### Single teacher, no verification

* Good, because it is the cheapest and simplest.
* Bad, because hallucinations land directly in training data.

### Judge from the same provider/family

* Good, because it simplifies billing and key management.
* Bad, because shared training data and alignment produce correlated blind
  spots: the judge confirms the teacher's mistakes with high confidence.

### Distinct provider and family (chosen)

* Good, because provider-level and family-level separation decorrelate the
  verifier from the generators.
* Bad, because it requires at least two accounts/keys.
* Bad, because "different family" relies on honest `family` declarations.

### Majority vote, no judge

* Good, because it needs no arbiter.
* Bad, because votes without reasoning are unauditable, and the canonical
  record (evidence, remediation, disagreement flag) would not exist.

### Human review of every record

* Good, because it is the ground truth.
* Bad, because it does not scale beyond the golden dataset (50-200 cases),
  which remains the phase-2 complement.
